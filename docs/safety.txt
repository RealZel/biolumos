Although photobiomodulation is generally considered a well-tolerated modality when used within appropriate parameters, side effects and misuse-related problems are still possible. The evidence base on adverse events is smaller than the mechanistic literature, so practical caution remains important.

Potential tissue overheating
PBM uses low-intensity light, but prolonged exposure, inappropriate contact conditions, or incorrectly selected output settings can still cause local overheating. This is particularly relevant near sensitive structures such as the eyes or injured skin. When PBM devices are used correctly, thermal injury appears uncommon, but exposure of the eyes to near-infrared light must always be avoided.

Incorrect dosing and paradoxical response
PBM is known for a biphasic dose response: too little energy may have no meaningful effect, while too much may reduce the desired effect or worsen tolerance. In acute inflammation, infection, or highly sensitive tissue states, poorly chosen parameters may aggravate discomfort instead of reducing it. This is why protocol selection, session timing, and treatment duration matter.

Practical precautions
- Consult a licensed physician before using the device, especially if you have an active medical condition.
- Do not direct the light toward the eyes.
- Do not use the device over areas with suspected malignancy unless specifically cleared by the treating physician.
- Use additional caution in pregnancy, in children, in patients with photosensitivity, and in people with active fever or acute infection.
- Stop use and seek medical advice if pain, redness, dizziness, or unusual discomfort increases.

Overall interpretation of the literature
Review papers and controlled studies generally describe PBM as safe when power density, dose, exposure time, and anatomical target are chosen correctly. At the same time, safety does not guarantee efficacy for every indication. Large clinical trials in some areas, such as acute stroke, have shown acceptable tolerability but no clinical benefit for the tested protocol. Safety therefore needs to be considered separately from effectiveness.
